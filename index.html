Introduction to Neural Network Interpretability

A structured, hands-on guide in four parts - from random machines to the inside of trained ones

Ruchir Tewari ruchirtewari@gmail.com

This book takes a close look at neural networks and their interpretability, with an objective of understanding model mechanics. It goes from mathematical foundations in Part I, to Representation geometry, Circuits, Control in Part II. Then it imposes the framework of Statics and Dynamics in Parts III and IV to interpret the internal static representations and dynamic representation changes at inference, closing with an analysis of in-context learning. Each book has a number of figures and detailed references to aid understanding visually and gives neuroscience parallels and pointers for further researh. Several experiments are performed on small LLMs and their detailed probes and experiment setups are described in Parts III and IV with annotated figures.

Part I - From Random Machines to Trained Machines

Chapters 1-4 . Model foundations

  1. Statistical Foundations - the statistical machine, the Markov property, entropy and cross-entropy as the training objective
  2. Geometric Foundations - Fisher information and the Cramér-Rao bound, Fisher information in trained networks, the Fisher-Rao metric, Shannon and Fisher compared
  3. Neural Networks as Probability Distribution Machines - transformers as conditional distribution shapers, attention as a directional relation extractor, flows and diffusion, mixtures of experts, instruments for the inside
  4. Training Dynamics - gradient, stochastic and natural gradient descent, K-FAC, the implicit geometric bias of SGD, training-time levers on interpretability, skip connections and the residual stream

Part II - Geometry, Circuits, Control, and Evidence

Chapters 5-8 . Interpretability foundations

  1. Representation Geometry - intrinsic dimension, superposition, the measured activation cloud, probes and lenses, interventions, conceptors
  2. Features, Circuits, and Causal Verification - sparse autoencoders, induction heads and the IOI circuit, activation patching and causal scrubbing
  3. Control and Steering - activation addition and the refusal direction, conceptors and affine steering, prompting, fine-tuning and steering compared
  4. Evaluation and Open Problems - faithfulness, information geometry and training dynamics, open problems and future tools

Appendix: a timeline of interpretability research, 2019-2026

Part III - Statics

Chapters 9-12 . What holds a representation in place, measured on GPT-2 small

  1. Forces: Writers, Taps and Equilibrium - heads and feedforward sublayers as writers on the residual stream, equilibrium of a representation, recording two sibling concepts
  2. Resultants: The Residual Stream as a Sum of Writes - exact attribution of a readout to its writers, parent and child concepts in one resultant, ellipsoids and a reading gate
  3. Inertia: Along Depth and Along Training - what carries a write upward, what the objective pins, the depth profile of a concept
  4. Movement: Steering and the Reach of One Pass - pushing the stream to test use, programs compiled with RASP and Tracr, sweeping a push around the concept plane

Conclusion: the findings and the open experiments. Appendix A: seven further probes (a push carried by the KV cache, cache-value and cache-key deltas, the ablation ladder, column masks, eviction, a rank-one weight edit, a removed direction and continued training). Appendix B: packing, readout and a survey of the literature by move.

Part IV - Kinematics

Chapters 13-16 . Representations in motion: what stays the same when the query, the store or the context changes

  1. Rigidity of Representations Under a Change of Query - shared representations across languages and models, a linear map split into rigid and non-rigid parts, rigidity by depth, head class and relation (measured)
  2. Constraints on the KV Cache - what a decode step writes and reads, multi-head latent attention and four other constraints, what survives compression (proposed)
  3. Training and Inference as Coupled Loops - four clocks, latent attention's two forms, the heal after cache conversion, expert routing, the attention sink (proposed)
  4. In-Context Learning as a Driven Response - induction and task heads, the in-context ridge estimator, settling under a steer and resonance across languages (measured)

Appendix A: the ridge map between caches, stores and weights. Appendix B: the measurements not run, and what each needs.

Read Part IV (PDF)

Each chapter ends with takeaways and runnable code; Parts I and II add open research directions scoped for a motivated reader to begin, and Parts III and IV report experiments run on GPT-2 small and Qwen3-0.6B. The full example suite runs on a laptop with Python and GPT-2-class models. Short "neuroscience parallel" interludes trace which methods the field inherited from systems neuroscience — and which failure modes came along for the ride.

Hands-on programs

The programs/ directory holds the runnable code for the four Parts, grouped by topic: part1_foundations and part2_representations (the chapter programs, from Markov’s letter statistics to induction heads, refusal-direction steering and a grokking phase transition; run them with programs/run.sh), part3_statics and part3_training (the Part III recordings, probes and retraining experiment on GPT-2 small), part4_dynamics (the Part IV rigidity and settling measurements on GPT-2 and Qwen3-0.6B), and kv_bridge, tracr and tools. See programs/README.md for the map and programs/README_programs.txt for what each chapter program does.
